FC26 Node Wiring

Pin-to-pin wiring for the two ESP32-C3 Super Mini nodes. Node 1 measures the PT100, Node 2 drives the 220V hooter. Node 2 makes its own Wi-Fi network (FC26-NET, no router) that Node 1 joins; the browser test page or the Flutter app talks to both nodes over BLE.

The pin order drawn is the common Super Mini layout with the USB-C at the top. Always go by the labels printed on your board. Tags like 3V3, GND and 5V mean "connect to that pin"; every GND on a node is the same wire.

2N2222 legs: most TO-92 parts (PN2222A) are E-B-C with the flat face toward you, legs down, but some (P2N2222A) are C-B-E. Check yours with a multimeter's diode mode: the base is the leg that reads ~0.6 V to both other legs (red probe on base).

Bare relay pins (SRD-05VDC-SL-C style): two coil pins, plus COM, NO and NC. Find the coil with a multimeter (~70 Ω between the two coil pins). Check the contact rating printed on the relay: it must be at least 250 VAC and more than the hooter's current.

⚠️ 220V: read before wiring either node

  • Switch the live (L) wire through the relay, never neutral. Use COM → NO, so the hooter is silent whenever the board is off or rebooting.
  • A 1 A fuse on live, before anything else, on each node. Node 1: mains L → fuse → Hi-Link AC L; mains N → Hi-Link AC N. Node 2: mains L → fuse, then from the fused side to both the Hi-Link AC L and the relay COM; mains N → Hi-Link AC N and the hooter.
  • Everything that touches 220V (Hi-Link input pins, relay contacts, fuse, hooter terminals) goes inside a closed plastic box. Use strain relief on the mains lead. Keep mains tracks/wires ≥ 6 mm away from the low-voltage side. If the hooter has a metal body or an earth terminal, connect it to mains earth.
  • Never connect USB while the board is on mains. Flash the firmware first with USB only, then unplug USB, then plug in mains.
  • Bench-test Node 2 with a bulb in place of the hooter first. Ask the venue whether a mains device on stage is allowed, and keep the plug within reach.
